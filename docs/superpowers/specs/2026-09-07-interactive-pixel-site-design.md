# Interactive Pixel-Art Personal Site — Design

Date: 2026-09-07
Repo: danielk927/doyoonkim.dev (public)

## Goal

A personal website you navigate by walking a character around a Hong Kong street
map with WASD or arrow keys, in the visual and movement idiom of the Gen 4
Pokémon DS games. Landmark buildings hold the content: Experience, Education,
Projects, About + Contact, Misc. Notes. Walk to a door, press `E`, a panel opens.

Success criteria:

1. A visitor lands, sees a character, and moves it with WASD/arrows unprompted.
2. Every landmark is reachable on foot from the spawn point.
3. Every resume section is readable in-game and on `/plain`.
4. Phones get `/plain`, a clean static resume page, not a broken canvas.
5. Movement speed is identical on a 60Hz and a 144Hz display.

## Non-goals

No NPCs, quests, battles, inventory, save state, audio, minimap, or interiors.
This is a walking sim over a resume.

## Legal constraint

No Pokémon assets. Gen 4 tilesets and sprites are Nintendo/Game Freak copyright
and this repo is public under the author's real name. The *style* is reproduced;
no asset is copied. All art is either CC0 or generated by `scripts/build_assets.py`.

## Achieving the Gen 4 look

The base tileset is Kenney's Roguelike Modern City (CC0, public domain). Four
things close the gap to Pearl, and three are free:

1. **De-outlining.** Kenney puts a hard near-black keyline on every sprite; Gen 4
   defines form through shading instead. `deoutline()` replaces thin dark lines
   with a darkened blend of their neighbours. A dark pixel only counts as an
   outline when 4+ of its 8 neighbours are bright, which protects large dark
   fills like asphalt from being eaten.
2. **Palette.** `gen4_palette()` lifts shadows (DS art is hazy, not inky), pulls
   hues warm and enriches saturation — but leaves near-neutral pixels neutral.
   Saturating greys turns pavement mint, which was the first attempt's failure.
3. **UI chrome.** The rounded, double-bordered dialogue window and its corner
   arrow are drawn by us, so they can be exact. This carries a large share of the
   impression at no asset cost.
4. **Grid-locked movement.** Pearl moves tile-to-tile with turn-in-place and a
   run toggle, not free-floating physics. This is engine code, it is instantly
   recognisable, and it is simpler than the AABB collision it replaces.

Generated additions to the sheet: harbour water, foam shoreline overlays, and
hanging neon shop signs. The player sprite is generated at Gen 4 proportions —
oversized head, ~1.25 tiles tall, no black keyline.

## Architecture

Next.js static export on Vercel. One `<canvas>` running a hand-written engine.
React never re-renders per frame: the engine is a plain class emitting events
(`promptChanged`, `openPanel`, `moved`) that React subscribes to for overlay UI.

```
src/
  engine/     Engine.ts input.ts grid.ts camera.ts renderer.ts mapParser.ts types.ts
  world/      map.ts tileset.ts
  content/    experience.ts education.ts projects.ts about.ts notes.ts
  components/ GameCanvas.tsx DialogueBox.tsx Panel.tsx PlainPage.tsx
  app/        page.tsx  plain/page.tsx
public/assets/  tileset.png character.png
```

### The map as ASCII

One continuous Hong Kong map authored as a template string, not in a map editor.
A legend in `world/tileset.ts` maps each character to a tile index and a `solid`
flag. Readable in a diff, editable without tooling, ~40-line parser.

Victoria Harbour splits the map: Kowloon north, Hong Kong Island south, joined by
a walkable pier crossing. Landmarks are marked with `[key]` and bound to content.

### Movement

Grid-locked, 16px tiles drawn at 2x (32px effective, DS scale).

- Tapping a direction the player is not facing **turns in place** without moving,
  as in Pearl. Holding it then walks.
- A walk step tweens over a fixed duration; input during a step is buffered so
  held keys chain smoothly.
- Shift runs (roughly 1.8x).
- `requestAnimationFrame` with a fixed-timestep accumulator, so speed does not
  vary with refresh rate.
- Camera follows the player, clamped to map bounds.
- Interaction: `E`/`Space` checks the tile the player faces for an interactable.

## Content: one source, two renderers

All copy lives once in `src/content/*.ts`. The in-game panels and `/plain` both
read it, so they cannot drift.

`/plain` is what phones get, what `<noscript>` points to, and what search and AI
crawlers read — canvas content is invisible to all of them.

Opening a panel pushes `?panel=<key>` so a section is directly linkable; loading
such a URL places the player at that landmark with the panel open.

### Privacy

The source resume carries a phone number. It is deliberately excluded from the
repo and the site, both public. Contact is email, LinkedIn, and GitHub.

## Mobile

Detected via `matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)')` plus a width check, not user-agent
sniffing. Coarse pointers render `PlainPage`, with a link to play anyway.

## Testing

Vitest over pure logic, written before implementation:

- ASCII map parser: dimensions, legend lookup, landmark extraction, ragged input.
- Grid movement: walkability, turn-in-place on a new facing, blocked steps,
  step tween completion, input buffering.
- Camera clamping: all four edges, and maps smaller than the viewport.
- Interaction: the faced tile resolves to the right landmark.
- Map integrity: every landmark key exists in content, every landmark is
  reachable from spawn (flood fill).

Rendering is verified in a browser, not unit-tested.
